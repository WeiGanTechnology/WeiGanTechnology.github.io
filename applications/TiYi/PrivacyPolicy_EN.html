Privacy Policy for TiYi

Last updated: 2026.10.17 · 简体中文

TiYi is a multi-protocol VPN client provided by Weigan Technology (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as "we" or "us"). This Privacy Policy applies to TiYi on iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and tvOS (Apple TV).

We attach great importance to the protection of your personal information and privacy. We will provide appropriate security measures for your personal information in accordance with legal requirements and industry-standard security practices.

This Privacy Policy works together with our User Agreement for TiYi.

TiYi is a VPN / proxy client that runs on your device and connects to nodes, remote configuration URLs, or enterprise VPN servers that you import yourself. We do not operate public VPN nodes and do not provide a traffic-relay service hosted by us. Profiles are stored primarily in the main app's local database. The compiled configuration used for the current connection, tunnel options, and some preferences are shared with the system Network Extension on the same device through an App Group. On Apple TV, those shared files are stored in the App Group's Library/Caches directory. IKEv2 passwords are stored in the system Keychain. You can unlock unlimited connection time with a subscription or one-time purchase processed by Apple. Without a purchase, daily connection time is limited. Apple TV and macOS currently do not integrate third-party advertising SDKs. iOS / iPadOS may show ads until unlimited connection time is unlocked. iCloud sync is off by default. Only after you turn it on, or run a manual sync, are profiles written to your own iCloud, not to servers we operate.

1. How We Collect and Use Your Information

We will only use your personal information where we have a lawful basis to do so. Under applicable law, we may process your information based on lawful grounds such as your consent, where necessary to perform or enter into a contract with you, or where necessary to comply with legal obligations.

1.1 Profiles, nodes, and remote configuration URLs. On iOS, iPadOS, and macOS, you may import a configuration by pasting text, entering a remote configuration URL, importing a file, opening a configuration file from the system, or using a URL scheme (such as ss, vmess, or vless). On iOS / iPadOS you may also scan a QR code. On Apple TV, you can type a remote configuration URL or configuration text with the remote, add a node manually (including WireGuard), enter IKEv2 details, or receive profiles after turning on iCloud sync on an iPhone or Mac. Apple TV does not offer camera scanning, system file import, or clipboard access. Supported protocols include Shadowsocks, VMess, VLESS, Trojan, Hysteria / Hysteria2, TUIC, WireGuard, OpenVPN, Clash / sing-box configurations, or share links, subject to your actual build. The original configuration, parsed node list, remote configuration URL, expiry information, and the node you select are stored locally on your device so that the app can connect, switch nodes, and display profiles. The compiled configuration needed for the current connection may be written to the App Group for the Network Extension to read. When you import or refresh a remote configuration, the app requests the URL you provided and downloads the configuration. That request goes to the third party you specified, not to servers we operate. The request may include a client identifier (the current User-Agent may contain fields such as clash-meta/tiyi, subject to your actual build) so the provider can recognize the client type.

1.2 VPN connection, traffic routing, and DNS. After you authorize the system VPN / Network Extension, the Service may establish a packet tunnel or a system IKEv2 connection on your device and forward traffic according to the rule, global, or direct mode you choose. On Apple TV, the system does not offer an include-all-networks switch. If you turn on LAN bypass, private and link-local addresses are sent direct by on-device tunnel rules. Web and app traffic inside the tunnel is forwarded by the operating system and the on-device engine to the node or VPN server you specified. Default configurations generated by the app for proxy connections may include third-party DNS resolver addresses (the current build may use 1.1.1.1, 223.5.5.5, or similar, subject to your actual build). A complete configuration you import yourself may also specify other DNS servers. During a connection, domain-name lookups may be sent to those resolvers, system DNS, or a node you configured. We do not record, store, or analyze your browsing content, access history, DNS queries, or full traffic logs on servers we operate, and we cannot view your browsing content from our servers.

1.3 IKEv2 credentials. If you add an IKEv2 profile, the username, server address, and identifiers are stored in the local profile; the password is written to the system Keychain and read by the system VPN API to establish the connection. We do not upload IKEv2 passwords to our servers. If you turn on iCloud sync or run a manual sync, that password is written with the profile into your iCloud container and stored by Apple.

1.4 Speed tests, latency checks, and traffic statistics. When you measure node latency in the app, the device makes a short TCP connection attempt to that node's host and port. Configurations generated by the app for proxy connections may also include an automatic urltest outbound that periodically probes the speed-test URL you configured (the default may be a public connectivity-check address such as https://www.gstatic.com/generate_204, which you can change in Settings). That request is typically sent through the relevant node and is handled by the connectivity-check provider under its own policies. Upload/download byte counts are calculated by the on-device Network Extension, shown in the app, and stored locally.

1.5 Diagnostic logs. During a connection, the local engine and Network Extension may generate diagnostic logs (such as connection status and error messages) so the app can show why a connection failed. Logs remain on your device. We do not automatically upload them to our servers.

1.6 Camera and clipboard (on your action). On iOS / iPadOS, after you grant camera access, the Service may scan a node or remote-configuration QR code and parse only the code you point at. When you tap Paste from Clipboard, the app reads the current clipboard text to import a configuration. We do not read or upload clipboard content in the background. Apple TV does not request or use the camera and does not read the clipboard. The current macOS build does not offer camera scanning.

1.7 File import and encrypted backup. On iOS, iPadOS, and macOS, when you choose to import a local file (such as `.json`, `.yaml`, `.yml`, `.ovpn`, `.conf`, or `.txt`), the app reads the configuration text from the file you selected. This read happens only after you choose the file. The content is stored locally on your device. On those platforms you may also export or import a backup encrypted with a password you choose. The backup can include profiles, routing, DNS, rules, and node latency records. You keep the password and the file; we do not receive it. Apple TV does not offer local file import or encrypted-backup import and export.

1.8 Update checks, review prompts, and advertising (by platform). The app may query Apple App Store public APIs to see whether an update is available. iOS, iPadOS, and macOS may use the system review prompt. Apple TV does not currently show a system review prompt. Those requests are handled by Apple. Apple TV and macOS do not integrate a third-party advertising SDK, do not request tracking permission, and do not collect advertising identifiers. On iOS / iPadOS, if you have not unlocked unlimited connection time, the app may use Google AdMob to show app-open ads and rewarded videos. Watching a rewarded video can extend that day's free connection time. Where required, we first ask for advertising-related consent through Google's consent flow. An advertising identifier (IDFA) is read only after you allow tracking, and only for advertising. We do not provide VPN profiles, credentials, or internet content to the advertising service.

1.9 In-app purchases and free connection time. The Service offers auto-renewable subscriptions (such as monthly or yearly, as shown in the app) and a one-time purchase, both processed by the Apple App Store. Through StoreKit we learn whether a purchase succeeded, whether a subscription is active, and the result of Restore Purchases, so we can unlock unlimited connection time. We do not collect your full payment card number, billing address, or Apple ID password. Without a purchase, the app counts, on the device, how long the tunnel is actually connected that day. The current free allowance is 5 minutes per day (as shown in the app). When it runs out, the connection stops. That counter stays on the device and is not uploaded to our servers. On iOS / iPadOS, a rewarded video can add more connection time for that day. Apple TV does not offer that option.

1.10 iCloud sync (when you turn it on). Automatic sync is off by default. After you turn it on, or when you run a manual sync while iCloud is available, the app writes profiles to the iCloud container of your Apple Account (currently Documents/Profiles in iCloud.com.weigantech.network). Each profile may include its name, type, original configuration text, compiled configuration, remote configuration URL, selected node, upload/download totals, expiry, and the IKEv2 server, identifiers, username, and password. Configuration text may contain node keys or certificates. Apple stores and transmits these files under Apple's iCloud policies. We cannot read your iCloud content from servers we operate. You can turn automatic sync off at any time, or disable iCloud for this app in system Settings.

1.11 What we do not do. We do not require you to create an in-app account operated by us, and we do not provide a cloud account operated by us. We do not enable VPN without your authorization, do not request photo library, contacts, microphone, location, Bluetooth, or other sensitive permissions unrelated to core connectivity (subject to your actual build), and do not upload your profiles, credentials, or internet content to our servers. Apple TV also does not use the camera, clipboard, notifications, tracking, or advertising identifiers.

2. Third-Party Services

This section describes third-party services that the Service may involve. Actual capabilities may differ by operating-system version.

2.1 Apple services (iOS / iPadOS / macOS / tvOS)

The Service uses Apple's Network Extension (Packet Tunnel), system VPN (including IKEv2), Keychain, App Groups, and StoreKit (in-app purchases and subscriptions), as well as optional iCloud Drive, camera, local network, notifications, App Store update lookup, and system review prompts. Camera, notifications, and the system review prompt are not used on Apple TV. VPN configurations are stored by the system and managed in system Settings. Purchases and subscriptions are processed by Apple. iCloud content stays in your Apple Account. Related processing is governed by Apple's policies.
Apple privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

2.2 Nodes, remote-configuration sources, DNS, and speed-test addresses

When you import or refresh a remote configuration, connect to a node, resolve a domain name, or run a speed test, network requests are sent to third-party servers you provide or select, and to third-party DNS / connectivity-check addresses in the app's default configuration (the current build may include 1.1.1.1, 223.5.5.5, and https://www.gstatic.com/generate_204, subject to your actual build). Those services are operated by the relevant providers. We cannot control their logging, retention, or privacy practices. Confirm that a source is trustworthy before importing it, and read that provider's terms and privacy policy.

2.3 Operating-system capabilities and the on-device engine

The Service uses an on-device VPN / proxy engine in the Network Extension to process profiles you import, and uses operating-system capabilities for local storage, Keychain, networking, file picking, camera, and UI rendering. Except for necessary requests to third parties you specify, to Apple (including iCloud and the App Store), or to the advertising service described below, we do not upload your profiles, credentials, or internet content to cloud servers we operate.

2.4 Google advertising services (iOS / iPadOS only)

Until unlimited connection time is unlocked, the iOS / iPadOS build may use Google AdMob and the related consent and ad-attribution capabilities. Apple TV and macOS do not use these services. Google's handling of advertising data is governed by Google's own policies.
Google privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

3. Paid Features and Commercialization

The current version offers auto-renewable subscriptions and a one-time purchase, both processed by Apple, to unlock unlimited connection time. Price, length, and name are as shown on the App Store and the purchase screen. A subscription renews automatically unless you turn off renewal in your Apple ID subscription settings at least 24 hours before the end of the current period. Apple may charge the renewal within 24 hours before the period ends. You can manage or cancel at any time in system Subscriptions. After cancellation, the period already paid for generally remains available until it ends. A one-time purchase does not renew. Purchases under the same Apple ID can be restored on the platforms this app supports, including Apple TV, subject to Apple's verification.

Without a purchase, daily connection time is limited. On iOS / iPadOS, ads may appear until you unlock unlimited time, and a rewarded video can extend that day's free time. After unlock, rewarded videos are no longer used to extend free time. Apple TV and macOS do not show third-party ads, and Apple TV cannot extend free time by watching a video.

4. Device Permission Usage

TiYi requests permissions only as needed for VPN connectivity, profile import, and speed testing. We ask for your consent through the system permission dialog or system-settings guidance. Permissions you deny will limit only the related features. Permissions are described by platform below.

iOS / iPadOS

VPN / Network Extension

Used to install and enable a packet tunnel or system IKEv2 configuration so the app can connect through nodes you import. Connection is not possible without this authorization.

Camera

Used to scan node or remote-configuration QR codes. You can still import profiles by paste, URL, or file if this permission is denied.

Local Network

Used for node latency checks and local-proxy related communication.

Internet

Used to import or refresh remote configurations, run speed tests, resolve domain names, check for App Store updates, and connect to nodes you specify.

Clipboard (on-demand read)

Read only when you tap paste-to-import.

Files (user-selected)

Used to read a local configuration file after you choose it and import its contents.

Notifications

Used to alert you when a node subscription is about to expire, traffic is low, or a remote-configuration refresh fails. Denying this permission does not block connections.

Tracking (on request)

Used only while unlimited connection time is locked, and only after you allow it, to read an advertising identifier for ads. You can still connect if you deny it. Ads may be less relevant, and whether a rewarded video is available depends on what is actually shown.

macOS

VPN / Network Extension, local network, internet, clipboard paste, file import, encrypted backup, and notifications are used for the same purposes described above. The current macOS build does not provide camera QR scanning and does not integrate a third-party advertising SDK. You can import profiles by paste, remote configuration URL, or file.

Apple TV (tvOS)

VPN / Network Extension is used to install a packet tunnel or system IKEv2 configuration so the app can connect through nodes you import or sync. Internet access is used for remote configurations, speed tests, domain-name resolution, iCloud sync, App Store purchase checks, and connecting to nodes you specify. If the system asks for local-network access, it is used for node latency checks and local-proxy related communication. Apple TV does not use the camera, clipboard, file picker, notifications, tracking, or advertising identifiers, and it does not import or export encrypted backup files. You can type a configuration with the remote, or sync it from an iPhone or Mac that has iCloud turned on.

You can disable VPN, withdraw camera, local-network, notification, or tracking access, turn off iCloud for this app, manage a subscription under your Apple ID, or uninstall the app at any time in system Settings to stop related processing.

5. Managing Your Personal Information

Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or object to certain processing of your personal information, and to data portability where applicable.

You can stop processing and remove locally stored profiles, preferences, and logs by deleting profiles in the app, removing the VPN configuration in system Settings, turning off iCloud sync, uninstalling the app, or clearing app data. IKEv2 passwords are removed from the Keychain when the corresponding profile is deleted. If that profile was already synced to iCloud, delete it while sync is on, or remove this app's iCloud data in system Settings. Cancel a subscription in your Apple ID subscription settings. For information processed by Apple, by Google (iOS / iPadOS ads only), or by a node, remote-configuration, DNS, or speed-test provider you specify, you may also exercise choices through that provider's settings and policies.

To exercise rights relating to data we control, contact us using the details in Section 7. We will respond within a reasonable time as required by applicable law.

6. Information Storage Location and Retention Period

6.1 Profiles, node lists, routing preferences, traffic statistics, the free-time counter, and diagnostic logs you generate through the app are handled and stored primarily on your device. The compiled configuration needed for the current connection and some preferences may be shared with the Network Extension through an App Group. On Apple TV, shared App Group files are kept in Library/Caches. IKEv2 passwords are stored in the system Keychain. We do not operate a cloud account. Only when you turn on iCloud sync or run a manual sync are the profiles described in Section 1.10 stored in your iCloud. How long they remain there depends on Apple and your iCloud settings.

6.2 Remote-configuration refresh, DNS resolution, speed tests, and tunnel traffic occur between your device and the relevant third parties. Update checks, purchase verification, and iCloud sync are processed by Apple according to Apple's policies and infrastructure, which may involve servers outside your country of residence. Ad requests on iOS / iPadOS are processed by Google under Google's policies and may also involve servers in other regions.

6.3 We retain information only for as long as necessary to fulfill the purposes described in this Policy, unless a longer retention period is required by law. After you uninstall the app, delete a profile, or clear data, corresponding local information is deleted, but VPN preferences already written to the system, and requests already sent to third parties, remain subject to those system or third-party mechanisms.
